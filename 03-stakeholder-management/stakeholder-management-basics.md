# Stakeholder Management for BA

Once you know what needs to change (requirements) and why (root cause), a BA has to manage the people affected by that change — this note covers the core frameworks for identifying, prioritizing, and communicating with stakeholders.

## What I'm trying to achieve here
Learn how to systematically map who matters on a project, how much influence/interest they have, and how to keep them aligned — instead of just reacting to whoever emails loudest.

---

## 1. Stakeholder Identification
List everyone affected by or influencing the project: end users, sponsors, approvers, IT/technical teams, external parties (e.g. vendors), and anyone whose process changes as a result.

*BA use:* On a Germany AP process change, stakeholders include vendors, the Germany finance team, SAP HANA/IT support, and internal helpdesk agents.

## 2. Power/Interest Grid (Stakeholder Mapping)
Plots stakeholders on two axes — power (ability to influence the project) and interest (how much they care about the outcome):
- **High power, high interest** → Manage closely, involve directly in decisions
- **High power, low interest** → Keep satisfied, don't overwhelm with detail
- **Low power, high interest** → Keep informed
- **Low power, low interest** → Monitor with minimal effort

## 3. RACI Matrix
Clarifies roles for each task/decision:
- **R**esponsible — does the work
- **A**ccountable — owns the outcome, signs off
- **C**onsulted — gives input before a decision
- **I**nformed — updated after a decision

*BA use:* For a process change proposal, a BA is often "Responsible" for documenting it, a finance manager is "Accountable" for approving it, IT is "Consulted" on feasibility, and helpdesk agents are "Informed" once it's live.

## 4. Communication Planning
Deciding, for each stakeholder group: what they need to know, how often, in what format (email update, dashboard, meeting), and who delivers it. High-power/high-interest stakeholders typically need more frequent, more detailed communication.

## 5. Managing Conflicting Requirements
When stakeholders want different things (e.g. Finance wants stricter invoice controls, vendors want faster processing), a BA's job is to surface the conflict early, quantify trade-offs where possible (using the stats techniques from note 02), and facilitate a decision rather than picking a side unilaterally.

- Draft a simple RACI for a process change so approval steps are clear upfront instead of discovered mid-project
