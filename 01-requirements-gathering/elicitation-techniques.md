# Requirements Elicitation Techniques

Requirements elicitation is how a Business Analyst discovers what stakeholders actually need — as opposed to what they first ask for. Good elicitation prevents scope creep and rework later in a project.

## Core techniques

**1. Interviews**
One-on-one conversations with stakeholders. Best for deep, specific input (e.g. a Finance Controller explaining exception handling in AP). Structured interviews use a fixed question set; unstructured interviews let the conversation follow the stakeholder's lead.

**2. Workshops / JAD sessions**
Group sessions bringing multiple stakeholders together to agree on requirements in real time. Useful when different departments (e.g. Procurement and Finance) need to align on a shared process.

**3. Document Analysis**
Reviewing existing SOPs, process documents, system reports, or past incident logs to infer requirements that stakeholders may not think to state explicitly.

**4. Observation (Job Shadowing)**
Watching a process actually being performed, rather than relying on how someone describes it. Often reveals workarounds and pain points that interviews miss.

**5. Surveys/Questionnaires**
Useful for gathering input from a large or distributed stakeholder group where individual interviews aren't feasible.

**6. Prototyping**
Showing a mock-up or draft (e.g. a sample dashboard) to get concrete feedback — people often find it easier to react to something visible than to describe requirements in the abstract.

## How I'd apply this in my own work
In AP/P2P, this maps directly onto things like: interviewing the Germany vendor helpdesk team about recurring payment-status queries (interview), or reviewing hold-reason logs before proposing a new hold-reduction process (document analysis).
