# Agile & Scrum for BA

Many BA roles today operate inside Agile/Scrum teams rather than producing one big upfront BRD/FRD. This note covers how the BA role adapts in that setting.

## What I'm trying to achieve here
Understand how requirements work gets broken down and delivered incrementally in Agile, since most modern BA job descriptions expect this.

---

## 1. Core Agile/Scrum Concepts
- **Sprint** — a fixed short cycle (usually 1–2 weeks) in which a set amount of work is planned and delivered
- **Product Backlog** — the full prioritized list of everything that could be built
- **Sprint Backlog** — the subset of backlog items committed to for the current sprint
- **Scrum roles** — Product Owner (owns the backlog/priorities), Scrum Master (facilitates process), Development Team

## 2. User Stories
The Agile equivalent of a small requirement, written from the user's perspective:
**Format:** "As a [role], I want [goal], so that [benefit]."
*Example:* "As a Germany AP vendor, I want to receive an instant payment-status update, so that I don't need to contact the helpdesk for routine queries."

**Acceptance Criteria** — specific, testable conditions attached to a user story that define when it's "done" (plays the same role an FRD's acceptance criteria does, but scoped to one small story instead of a whole system).

## 3. Backlog Grooming / Refinement
An ongoing activity where the BA (often alongside the Product Owner) reviews upcoming backlog items, clarifies requirements, breaks large items down into smaller ones, and re-prioritizes based on new information.

## 4. The BA's Role in Agile Ceremonies
- **Sprint Planning** — clarifies requirements/acceptance criteria for stories being pulled into the sprint
- **Daily Stand-up** — BA may attend to stay aware of blockers related to requirements
- **Sprint Review/Demo** — BA validates that delivered work actually meets the original business need
- **Retrospective** — BA can contribute process-improvement input, similar to RCA thinking applied to the team's own way of working

## 5. Agile vs. Traditional (Waterfall) BA Work
In traditional/Waterfall projects, a BA typically produces a full BRD/FRD upfront before development starts. In Agile, requirements are captured as user stories and refined continuously, sprint by sprint, so a BA's documentation work is smaller, more frequent, and evolves as the team learns.

---

## How I'd apply this in my own work
If the Germany helpdesk automation project were run in Agile, I'd break it into stories like: "As a helpdesk agent, I want standard payment-status queries auto-answered, so I can focus on exceptions" — then define acceptance criteria for what counts as a "standard" query, and refine that definition sprint by sprint as edge cases surface.
